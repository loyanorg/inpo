# Anchor document — non-negotiable principles in under a page

**What it is:** `docs/ANCHOR.md`, a list of 7 to 12 statements that settle arguments before they start.

## Take

- Under 60 lines. If it grows past a page, principles are being mixed with guidelines; move guidelines out.
- Each principle is one declarative sentence plus at most two lines of consequence. Example: "Users are professionals. Consequence: no explanatory copy inside screens; clarity comes from layout and naming."
- Principles are stated as choices between two reasonable things ("Correct over fast", "Tablet is a first-class device, equal to desktop"), not as virtues nobody disputes ("Quality matters").
- Each principle is numbered so it can be cited: "per ANCHOR 4".
- Includes an explicit "we do not" list of 3 to 5 items, matching the Rejected list in this library where relevant.
- Change requires the owner; the file header says so and carries the date of the last change.
- Agents are instructed (in AGENTS.md) to quote the relevant principle number when a decision could go either way, and to stop and ask when a request contradicts one.
- Written in plain words; no slogans, no metaphor.

## Leave

- Mission statements and values pages aimed at customers or recruits.
- Technical conventions (lint rules, folder names); those live in CONTRIBUTING or lint config.

## Evidence

- Shape Up (URL above) on fixed appetite and boundaries as decision tools.
- Amazon Leadership Principles as the public example of numbered, citable tenets (no link kept; public as generally known).
