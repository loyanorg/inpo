# GOV.UK content style — plain words, one idea per sentence, answer first

**What it is:** the writing rules behind the UK government's websites, tested for reading speed and comprehension at scale.

## Take

- Front-load the answer: the first sentence states what the user can do or what happened. Background, if any, comes after.
- One idea per sentence. Target sentences under 25 words; split any sentence with "and" joining two actions.
- Plain words: use "start" not "commence", "buy" not "purchase", "help" not "assist". Keep a short list of banned words per project.
- No metaphor, no idiom, no humour in interface text.
- Active voice, second person: "Enter the invoice date", not "The invoice date should be entered".
- Labels are nouns or short noun phrases ("Due date"); buttons are verb phrases that name the outcome ("Save invoice", "Issue credit note"), never "OK" or "Submit".
- Numbers as digits, including 1 to 9. Dates as `1 October 2026`; times in 24-hour clock where the audience uses it.
- Headings are statements or questions a reader would ask, in sentence case. No title case.
- Error messages name the field and say what to do: "Enter a quantity greater than 0", not "Invalid input".

## Leave

- UK-specific spellings and date formats if the project's locale differs; keep the rules, change the locale.
- Hint text under every field. For professional users, add hints only where testing shows confusion (see Rejected list on explanatory copy).

## Evidence

- Style guide at the URL above; the A to Z section lists word choices.
- Quote: "Research shows that the longer a sentence is, the harder it is to read."
